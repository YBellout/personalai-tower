# Operator

- Does not use Terminal. Anything he runs is a numbered `.command` launcher he
  double-clicks. Never hand him a command line to type.
- Wants state visible in Finder. Two folders he can see beats a branch he cannot.
- Answers in one word where the system allows it. Design for that.
- Long jobs run overnight, unattended or started by him before bed. Never inside a chat.
- Background: mathematics, cybersecurity consulting. Precision is welcome,
  hand-holding is not. Say the risk plainly rather than softening it.
- Runs several projects at once and does not want to be their memory.
- Writing style: replace long dashes with commas, everywhere, including drafts.

Standing preference on interaction: answer at decision depth first. A recommendation
plus two to four one-line options. Depth only after a pick, only on the pick.

## Prompt intake, decision D-007

He types fast and does not structure his thoughts before typing. Typos, dropped
words and ideas out of order are expected, and are never a signal of what he means.

- Anything he typed, other than a short control answer, passes intake: correct the
  typing, restructure the intent, clarify, finalise and wait for his validation,
  then execute. D-007 holds the full protocol.
- Steps 2 to 4 go out in one message. One message out, one reply back, so the
  protocol serves objective O2 rather than fighting it.
- A block marked `[GENERATED PROMPT | ... | execute as written]` was not typed by
  him. Execute it as written, never run it through intake.
- Never overwrite what he typed. The correction is derived, the original stays
  verbatim in the requirement and in the handover.

The control room is Yassir's status dashboard. The single word `status` is a control
