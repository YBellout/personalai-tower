# D-007  Prompt intake protocol, and marking generated prompts

    Scope:       global
    State:       answered
    Decided by:  Yassir, 2026-09-20
    Supersedes:  nothing

## Context

Yassir types fast and does not structure his thoughts before typing, so messages
arrive with typos, dropped words and ideas out of order, and the live risk is that
Claude acts on a misread instead of surfacing it.

Separately, prompts the Tower generates are copied and pasted between projects, and
a receiving project currently cannot tell a generated prompt from something Yassir
typed, so it would run a correction pass over text that was already finalised.

## Options

1. Full intake on every message, four separate exchanges. Correct, but it turns a
   one-word pick into a round trip and breaks objective O2.
2. **Recommended, and taken.** Full intake on substantive input, steps 2 to 4
   delivered in one message, short control answers exempt. Same protection, one
   reply from him, O2 intact.
3. Spelling correction only, no restructure and no validation gate. Cheapest, but
   it fixes the typing without fixing the misread, which is the actual risk.
4. Status quo. Rejected by the requirement itself.

## Reversibility

All four are instruction text and fully reversible. Changing this decision costs one
edit here, one in `knowledge/operator.md`, and a re-paste of the block into every
governed project. Today that re-paste cost is zero, because no project has been
linked yet, which makes this the cheapest moment the change will ever have.

The exemption threshold in option 2 is the only judgement call in it. It is Claude's
call under the reversible-decisions rule, and widening or narrowing it costs one line.

## Cost of not deciding

Every fast-typed requirement stays a coin flip between a correct read and silent
rework, and the cost surfaces after the work is finished rather than before it
starts. On a project running unattended overnight, a misread requirement is a whole
night of confidently wrong work, found in the morning.

## The decision

### Rule 1, generated prompts are marked

Any prompt the Tower or a governed project produces for Yassir to copy and paste is
a fenced block whose first line is exactly:

    [GENERATED PROMPT | <project-id> | <YYYY-MM-DD> | execute as written]

The marker sits inside the block so it survives the paste, which is what lets the
receiving project recognise it. Anything in a reply not carrying that line was
typed by Yassir, and no other content is formatted to look like one.

### Rule 2, typed input passes intake

Text Yassir typed, other than a short control answer, passes five steps:

1. **Correct.** Spelling, grammar, obvious typos and dropped words. Never change
   meaning, and never change a name, number, path, id or quoted string. A correction
   that would change meaning is not made, it becomes a question in step 3.
2. **Restructure.** Restate the intent as a structured requirement: what he wants,
   why, and what done looks like. His own words are kept verbatim wherever they
   carry the requirement.
3. **Clarify.** The questions that prune the most, at most three, per the existing
   standard. Anything assumed instead of asked is listed and marked reversible.
4. **Finalise and validate.** Present the finished prompt or requirement. Nothing
   executes until he validates it.
5. Then **5.1 execute**, or, if he comments rather than validating, **5.2 run that
   comment back through steps 1 to 4** and return to step 4.

### Three clauses that make it work rather than cost

- **Steps 2, 3 and 4 ship in one message.** One message out, one reply back. Split
  into three exchanges it would triple his interaction cost and defeat objective O2,
  two human steps per requirement.
- **A generated prompt never enters intake.** A block carrying the Rule 1 marker was
  already corrected, restructured and validated. Running a correction pass over a
  prompt the Tower wrote is the specific failure this clause prevents.
- **The original is never overwritten.** The correction is a derived artefact. What
  he actually typed stays verbatim in the requirement's own-words field, per design
  doc section 15, and in the handover record.

### When intake does not fire

A short control answer executes directly: a pick ("B", "option 3"), a yes or no, a
go-ahead, a one-line correction of something already on the table, or anything under
roughly fifteen words that is unambiguous in the current context. Claude's call,
reversible, say the word to widen or narrow it.

## Worked example, this decision's own requirement

Step 1, corrected, meaning untouched:

> Establish a rule, persistent in this project and all governed projects: I am a
> fast typer and I type fast without structuring my thoughts.

Step 2, one thing the restructure surfaced that the raw text hid: the five steps as
typed were numbered 1, 2, 2, 4, 5.1, 5.2, with two steps sharing the number 2. They
are renumbered 1 to 5 above with no step dropped. That is exactly the class of error
the protocol exists to catch before work starts rather than after.
